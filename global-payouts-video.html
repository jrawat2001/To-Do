02 Why paying out is harder

Accepting money vs. sending it

Accepting a card payment
Sending a payout
Credentials
Customer brings standardized card details
Sender collects bank or wallet details in each country's format
Routing
One global card network routes it
Many local rails, each with its own rules
Compliance
Focus is on the paying customer
Sender verifies, screens and tax-reports the recipient
Reversibility
Chargebacks let disputes be reversed
Push payments are mostly final
Liquidity
Money flows in to you
Pre-fund the right currency in each market first

10 The banker's lens

Build, buy or partner?

Build

Licences country by country (in the U.S., money transmitter licences state by state), bank partners, compliance teams, years of integrations.

Buy

Buys coverage, licences and teams now. The logic behind Bridge, BVNK and Payoneer.

Partner

Plug into Stripe, Adyen or a specialist. Fastest, but the economics are shared.

11 Recap

Five things to remember

1

A payout is a chain. KYC → tax → FX → rail → delivery.

2

Paying out is harder than accepting. The sender owns compliance, liquidity and fraud risk.

3

Fees, FX spread, software and float. Margins hinge on direct connections and rates.

4

Stablecoins and instant payouts are the big shifts. Card networks and Stripe are buying in.

5

Consolidation is under way. Licences, coverage and recurring revenue earn the premium.
